Earth Science Key Concept

A slump is a cohesive block of soil that rotates backward as it slides along a concave failure surface. Groundwater adds weight and reduces friction between soil grains.

Tip: Increase rain or angle to trigger failure! Part 1 of 7

What is Rotational Slumping?

Unlike rapid rockfalls or mudflows, a rotational slump moves as a single cohesive mass along a spoon-shaped (concave) shear plane. Notice how trees tilt backward toward the top cliff as the block rotates!
